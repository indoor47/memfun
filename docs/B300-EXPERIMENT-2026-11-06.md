# B300 concurrency experiment (2026-11-06)

How much of one B300 does the swarm actually use, and what buys more
throughput: more agents per task, or more tasks in parallel?

## Setup

- Model: Qwen3.8-27B FP8 on SGLang, one B300, no Anthropic key. Every role
  (decomposer, worker, auditor) used the same model.
- Workload: the six toy smoke bundles (control-calc, formats-2, kvstores-4,
  minicli-3, textkit-3, validators-4), graded by hidden tests in a separate
  grader directory (`evals/swarm/run_smoke.py`).
- One trial per bundle. Arms A to C ran the six bundles once, six at a time.
  Arm E ran four copies of each, 24 at a time.
- Server metrics came from a sampler on SGLang's metrics endpoint
  (`*-sampler.csv`). Token counts come from the server counters and agree with
  the per-episode `llm.call` totals.

| arm | `--max-agents` | tasks in parallel | episodes | episode timeout |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1 | 6 | 6 | 25 min |
| B | 4 | 6 | 6 | 25 min |
| C | 8 | 6 | 6 | 25 min |
| E | 4 | 24 | 24 | 25 min (the run ended at about 18 min) |

## Results

| arm | hidden tests passed | episodes with all tests passing | running requests avg / peak | generation tok/s avg / peak | prompt / generated tokens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 88% (149/170) | 1 of 6 | 4.4 / 6 | 381 / 764 | 5.0M / 0.58M |
| B | 92% (131/142) | 3 of 6 | 5.8 / 14 | 453 / 1171 | 5.9M / 0.67M |
| C | 94% (160/170) | 1 of 6 | 6.4 / 12 | 495 / 1325 | 7.1M / 0.72M |
| E | 82% (533/652) | 9 of 24 | 28 / 55 | 1483 / 3030 | 13.4M / 1.45M |

Every episode in every arm ran into its timeout, so the numbers are "state at
cutoff", and wall-clock time is not comparable between arms.

## Findings

1. **Many tasks in parallel is the real lever.** Going from 6 to 24 tasks
   raised average generation throughput 3.9x (381 to 1483 tok/s) and peak
   throughput 4x. GPU time per task fell about 5x (250 s to 46 s of wall-clock
   per task), roughly $0.62 to $0.12 per task at the implied pod rate of about
   $9 an hour.
2. **More agents per task helps a little, then plateaus.** Arm C (8 agents)
   passed the most hidden tests, 94% against 88% for one agent, for 42% more
   prompt tokens. Treat the size of that gap with care: the arms were graded
   on different test totals (B: 142, others: 170) because some units produced
   no gradable output, and episodes with every test passing do not rise
   steadily (1, 3, 1 of 6). With one trial per arm this is a hint, not a
   measurement.
3. **The swarm loop is mostly serial** (decompose, work, review, revise). In
   arms A to C the server averaged only 4 to 6 running requests, so the GPU sat
   idle most of the time. Only extra parallel tasks filled it.
4. **About 90% of tokens are prompt tokens**, and the server's prefix cache
   served about 60 to 62% of them in every arm.
5. **SGLang ran triton attention and triton mamba backends at about 45 tok/s
   per stream.** That is a tuning lever we have not tried yet.
6. **Arm E lost some quality to load.** Hidden-test pass rate fell to 82%
   (arms A to C: 88 to 94%), and 4 of 24 episodes ended as model failures (none in arms A and
   C, one in B). Part of that may be the shorter 18-minute window.

## Caveats

- `--max-agents 1` caps the worker pool; it does not turn the swarm into a
  single agent. Arm A still ran a decomposer, workers and an auditor in
  sequence (11 agent invocations in the first episode).
- Reviewer failures cost some episodes sub-tasks. The first control-calc
  episode shows the auditor's answer lacking the required findings JSON block
  with the per-dispatch review id, so the sub-task was rejected after a retry.
  This comes from the fail-closed auditor change and lowers every arm's
  numbers somewhat. It is worth counting before the next run.
- The `edit_only` guard blocked `write_file` on existing files and the model
  retried with `edit_file`; one worker also ran `python` where only `python3`
  exists. Both are small costs, but they add up under a 25-minute cap.
- Tool usage is known for only 1 to 4 episodes per arm, so it says little.
- The server counters include any other traffic on the pod.

## What to do next

Run a real benchmark with one agent per task and many tasks in parallel, with a
timeout long enough for tasks to finish. See the Commit0-lite plan.
Also try a faster attention backend if SGLang supports one for this model.
